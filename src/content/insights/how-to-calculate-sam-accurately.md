Standard Allowed Minutes (SAM) or Standard Minute Value (SMV) is the single most critical number in an apparel manufacturing facility. It dictates piece rates, line balancing, factory efficiency metrics, and buyer cost quotations.

Yet, an alarming number of factories continue to calculate SAM using uncalibrated stopwatch time studies or historical guesses.

### The Flaws of Pure Stopwatch Studies
1. **Operator Pacing Bias:** Operators naturally adjust their working speed when being observed by a stopwatch engineer.
2. **Failure to Deconstruct Micro-Motions:** Stopwatch timing measures total elapsed seconds without identifying whether the motion pattern was optimized.
3. **Inconsistent Fatigue Allowances:** Allowances are often added arbitrarily (e.g. standard 15%) without evaluating garment weight, fabric slipperiness, or posture strain.

### The Power of PMTS & GSD
Predetermined Motion Time Systems (PMTS) and General Sewing Data (GSD) break every operation down into predefined elements:
* Get and place part
* Align seam edges
* Foot on pedal & stitch run
* Reposition and rotate
* Trim and dispose

By engineering the motion method *before* timing the process, factories consistently recover between 8% to 15% in baseline SAM across complex styles.
